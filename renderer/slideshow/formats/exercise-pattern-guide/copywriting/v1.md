# exercise-pattern-guide Copywriting v1

## Scope

This version governs English copy for `slideshow/exercise-pattern-guide`. The format turns one bounded strength-training decision into either an ordered decision process or a compact movement-pattern system. It owns language roles, ordering, density, and progression—not exercise truth, evidence admission, project geometry, imagery assets, or product claims.

The primary designated reference is the ordered eight-image post `Dba9dAICIfF`. The adjacent same-account posts `DYmE3IWCEdq` and `DcvsAmNCBJq` supply secondary evidence for editorial hierarchy and sequence rhythm only. Their wording, medical claims, credentials, branding, calls to action, and topic-specific structures are not reusable.

## Evidence gate

Define one practical decision that can be answered by a small, defensible set of movement or exercise patterns.

- Do not call the set the `best`, `only`, `perfect`, `golden`, or universally necessary exercises.
- Every named pattern, trained region, use case, and option must agree with accepted expertise and the depicted movement.
- Do not imply that a finite list covers every goal, muscle, limitation, or population.
- State the governing selection principle in plain language and preserve important setup, population, injury, and goal limitations.
- Use fewer cards rather than padding the system with a weak or mismatched pattern.
- The reference images are execution evidence, not proof of the exercise claims or image-label accuracy they contain.

## Sequence

Choose one sequence from the audience-facing information operation. Do not mix process steps with catalog cards merely to fill a fixed count.

1. **Cover:** name the bounded decision and its practical outcome. Follow with one short contrast, limitation, or organizing principle.
2. **Decision process:** use four to five ordered decision steps. Each slide performs one action, adds one practical rule, and advances the same decision toward resolution.
3. **Pattern system:** when the decision genuinely requires a catalog, use one rationale card and four to six distinct movement or exercise-pattern cards. The primary reference uses six pattern cards; use that count only when six defensible, non-overlapping patterns exist.
4. **Resolution:** the final step or pattern completes the decision. Do not add a recap, CTA, product insert, or motivational outro unless it performs a new information operation.

## Repeated-card language

For a decision process, each repeated card uses these ordered roles:

1. one short action or decision title;
2. one or two plain-language sentences that state what to do and why it matters;
3. no exercise-name list unless identifying the decision object requires one.

For a pattern system, each repeated card uses these ordered roles:

1. one familiar pattern or decision name, without an ordinal counter;
2. one plain-language definition or decision rule;
3. two or three accurately named exercise examples when examples are required to make the distinction concrete.

Keep definitions and decision rules concise enough to read at phone scale. Exercise names include setup qualifiers only when they prevent ambiguity. Examples illustrate the named distinction; they are not ranked recommendations or interchangeable prescriptions.

Do not add coaching instructions, dosage, unsupported mechanisms, transformation promises, lifestyle analogies that overstate transfer, or a second explanatory paragraph. Move a necessary caveat into the caption when it cannot fit without breaking the repeated card rhythm.

## Voice and rhythm

- Use direct, familiar U.S. strength-training language.
- Sound precise and editorial rather than motivational, clinical, or credential-led.
- Prefer concrete actions such as `sit down and stand up`, `pull toward you`, or `lift from the floor` over anatomy jargon when the simpler description is accurate.
- Use selective emphasis for the decision-bearing phrase; do not bold whole paragraphs.
- Keep repeated labels and sentence shapes stable while varying only the content required by each pattern.
- Omit counters, eyebrows, `Options:` labels, and other microcopy when the sequence, title, or image labels already communicate the same information.
- Do not imitate the source's distinctive title, phrasing, sign-off, or authority claims.

## Title and caption

Provide a separate concise title that restates the bounded practical decision. The caption names the organizing principle, one important limitation, and any evidence context that cannot fit naturally on the slides. During prelaunch, use no CTA and make no app or product claim.
