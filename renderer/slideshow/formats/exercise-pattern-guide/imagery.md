## Visual grammar

- Use a quiet white or warm off-white `1080 × 1350` editorial canvas with generous negative space.
- Pair a high-contrast editorial serif display face with a neutral sans-serif body face. Use the serif for cover hierarchy, card titles, and occasional resolution lines; use sans-serif for definitions, bullets, option labels, citations, and exercise labels.
- Keep black or near-black as the dominant ink and one neutral gray for secondary copy. Do not add a separate accent color when the exercise artwork already supplies anatomical red; the complete slide should read as one restrained black/gray system with the artwork providing the only chromatic emphasis.
- Keep every audience-facing element below the upper `10%` of the canvas so Instagram cropping cannot cut into the first visible line. The lower composition may extend closer to the edge when needed, but decisive labels remain clear of the interface zone.
- Omit counters, ordinal numbers, eyebrows, rules, boxes, arrows, and other micro-elements unless one performs a necessary information operation that the title, copy, and exercise artwork do not already perform. Include only the canonical account-wide `LIFT CODE` badge owned by `context/imagery.md`; do not reproduce the source account URL, wordmark, signature, medical credential, or any other branded footer.
- Preserve asymmetry and open margins. Do not turn the system into a dense dashboard, boxed template, dark gym poster, or commercial fitness ad.

## Cover

The cover combines a large left- or upper-weighted serif title with one dominant anatomical, movement, or equipment illustration entering from an edge. Use selective italic, weight, or accent color to articulate the title hierarchy. Place the short framing copy in a compact block beneath or beside the title and leave substantial empty field around both.

The hero illustration supports the named movement system without acting as scientific proof or a physique outcome claim. Do not reuse source anatomy art, source composition, or a recognizable source subject.

## Rationale or first-step card

Use a text-led editorial page: one large serif question, framing statement, or first decision step and one or two short sans-serif paragraphs. Add a diagram or character action only when it carries a relationship the copy cannot communicate as directly. Keep at least half the page visually quiet and do not decorate the empty field.

## Repeated cards

Choose the image operation from the copy rather than from whichever exercise assets are easiest to place.

For a decision process, each repeated card keeps this hierarchy:

1. one action or decision title at the top;
2. one compact practical rule below it;
3. one recurring character scene in the lower half that visibly performs the action: inspect, choose, move an item forward, organize the remainder, establish a default, or check a repeated record.

Remove exercise comparison labels, paired demonstrations, and arrows when the slide teaches a process rather than comparing movements. Props may identify the decision object without becoming a false exercise comparison.

For a movement-pattern system, each repeated card keeps this hierarchy:

1. pattern or decision name at the top, with no ordinal counter;
2. one compact definition or decision rule below it;
3. two or three exercise illustrations across the lower half, each with one short label.

Use original, generated, or rights-clear exercise illustrations that accurately depict the named option. Favor clean cutouts, restrained anatomy emphasis, and enough equipment context to distinguish setup. Two-option cards may use a balanced two-column arrangement; three-option cards may use three columns or one larger plus two smaller figures when labels remain legible.

The number of figures follows the copy, not a fixed slide template. Match every figure to its label before rendering. Reject cropped equipment, impossible contact points, malformed anatomy, unrelated muscle highlights, mislabeled movement phases, and illustrations that contradict the option name.
