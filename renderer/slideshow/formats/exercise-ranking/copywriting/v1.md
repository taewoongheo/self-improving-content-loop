# exercise-ranking Copywriting v1

## Scope

This version governs English copy for `slideshow/exercise-ranking`. The format helps a lifter compare or prioritize a bounded set of exercises for one named decision. It owns language roles, ordering, density, and progression—not exercise truth, scoring evidence, product claims, geometry, or imagery policy.

The designated source post shows a cover followed by individually ranked exercise cards with short rationales. Its exact exercise set, scores, wording, personal physique claim, and product insert are not reusable evidence.

## Evidence gate

Define one comparison question and one decision-relevant criterion supported by accepted expertise.

- Do not rank by vague overall quality, muscle growth, safety, or necessity.
- Do not convert personal preference into a universal score.
- If numeric scores are used, define scale anchors and apply them consistently. Prefer ordinal positions or bounded categories when evidence does not justify precision.
- Preserve relevant setup, population, technique, injury, and goal limitations.
- Use fewer items rather than padding the ranking with weak comparisons.

## Sequence

Use one cover followed by four to seven item slides. Omit a separate recap unless it adds a distinction not already visible.

1. **Cover:** name the bounded exercise set and exact comparison criterion.
2. **Item slides:** show one exercise or setup, its position or grade, and one concise rationale tied to the criterion.
3. **Resolution:** the final item may be the strongest recommendation only when the same evidence standard supports it.

The source's personal starting-physique slide is not reusable proof. Use personal context only when genuine first-party evidence materially explains the criterion. Omit product inserts during the current no-product prelaunch phase.

## Item language

Each item slide uses no more than three primary roles:

1. compact position or grade;
2. precise exercise name with meaningful setup qualifiers;
3. one bounded rationale in one or two short sentences.

Do not add a second coaching paragraph, unsupported mechanism, transformation promise, or universal programming rule. A concise caveat may replace the rationale when the limitation is the decision value.

## Voice and rhythm

- Lead with a concrete comparison understood before inspecting the image.
- Use direct, familiar U.S. gym language without imitating the source.
- Sound experienced but non-authoritarian: confident enough to compare and explicit about scope.
- Avoid rage-bait whose only mechanism is a provocative score.
- Vary sentence rhythm while keeping label grammar stable.
- Let ordering create progression; omit filler transitions.

## Title and caption

Provide a separate concise title. The caption states the criterion, one important limitation, and context that cannot fit naturally on slides. During prelaunch, use no CTA and make no app claim.
